Sermon transcript

Full transcript of the message preached by Jacobo Castillo.

Let us stand to read a passage of Scripture: John 20:21 through 23. We are in the most important week of the year, and that is because it is Contact Week. You have it, church.

John 20:21 through 23 says: “Jesus said to them again” (as if Jesus had already said it before, right? So He said it again): “Peace to you. As the Father sent Me, I also send you.” And when He had said this, He breathed on them and said, “Receive the Holy Spirit.” Listen to what it says: “If you forgive anyone’s sins, they are forgiven; if you retain anyone’s sins, they are retained.” Someone say, “I have power.”

Beloved God, thank You for Your blessed Word, which gives direction to Your beloved church. We pray for every need in this place, and we believe that this God we are preaching about is healing, is working, is already ministering to us, and is also empowering us to do the work to which we were called and established in this city. Lord, we give You honor and glory in the name of Jesus. Amen. Glory to God. You may take your seats, shouting, “Glory to God!” Amen. Glory to Christ Jesus. Blessed be His name. We give You praise, Lord Jesus.

I want to preach to you under the title “The Power of Making Contact.”

As I was telling you, we are in one of the most important weeks of the vision. We have already written down seven friends and family members whom we will contact this week. Yesterday I was interviewing people for the ministry and noticed something in common. They said, “Eight years ago, God reached me and changed my life. We are here because we came to Christ ten years ago, and now we preach the gospel. We serve God. Our lives changed because there is power in the gospel of Jesus.” There is power in the gospel of Jesus. Praise the Lord.

God has commissioned the church to carry the message of the gospel. The message that changed us will change the lives of our families. It will also change the lives of our children and our neighbors. Amen, beloved church.

And those people we wrote down during these five weeks... In the first week, last week, we prayed and wrote their names down. This week we are going to text or call these seven people and say, “How are you? How have you been?” God has already placed one family on my heart, and I told my wife, “Let’s reach out to this family.” The mother in this family used to worship with us before returning to her country, and I am sure she is still praying for her son here. So I want to be part of what God wants to do to reach that family.

Today we are going to learn about the power of making contact. In week three, we will visit those families. How many of you remember God’s visit to your lives? We will enter new homes. We will go to new households. We will take the gospel to those homes. Blessed be the Lord Jesus. Glory to God. In the fourth week, we will invite them. We will have some beautiful invitations and take them to the families or leave them in the mail so they can come to our fifth week, which is Friend Day. We will make that first contact by inviting them to a spiritual gathering: Friend Day.

I still remember; I will never forget what Friend Day can do. There are people who cannot come to a church because there is a battle in their soul, because they feel disappointed in the church or disappointed in themselves. But when we use the word “celebration,” and the church has already prayed and fasted, a time of celebration can also be a spiritual celebration. I have witnesses here. I said, I have witnesses here. God can work at a Friend Day celebration, just as He has worked in us. How many came to church through a Friend Day? Raise your hand. Have you attended a Friend Day? Praise the Lord. We will be giving everyone the invitations soon.

But what does it mean to make contact? It means establishing contact or communication with someone, with us also serving as a transmitter. We are used as transmitters, or transistors. We do not change anyone; we have no power of our own. But we are connected to the source, who is Christ Jesus. When we make contact with someone, the power of the gospel transforms and blesses them. When we reach those families, the power of the gospel touches their lives. We are that contact with God.

We read in John that we are filled with the Holy Spirit. Say it: We are filled with the Holy Spirit so that other people may receive forgiveness for their sins from God. But John also says that if we do not make contact, those people will not be able to encounter Jesus. Before we leave this place today, the importance of our role in this journey between them and God must settle into our hearts.

In the Bible, there are only two places where we should position ourselves. One is the sower, and the other is the soil where the seed falls so it can bear fruit. We are either sowers or soil; we cannot be in any other place. We are sowers or soil. One day we were soil where someone planted the Word, and the Word worked in us. If we open our mouths, someone will receive blessings from God. If we open our mouths, people will meet the God we have met. We are simply a very important point of contact.

The Bible tells us a story in Acts chapter 8 about a person, a church member named Philip... If you have not read it in the Bible, you will realize when you do that God took him seriously. God took him from where he was, in the middle of a revival, to make contact with a key man whom the Bible calls a eunuch. That man was returning to his city after coming to worship God, but he did not know God. And God wanted to reach him. So God took Philip and transported him to another place. One day we will be transported. Glory to the Lord. We will move to another place. And God took him there, where the Bible says the man was traveling in his chariot—not like the Mercedes-Benz you drive. No, not one of those. It was a chariot pulled by horses. At that moment, Philip understood that God had not taken him there by chance; he had a work of making contact to do.

Beloved church, our lives are not meant only for enjoying God’s blessings. We are meant to make contact with people so they can know the God we preach. Our visits, wherever we go, are sent by God with a purpose. We are where we are for a purpose. We go where we go for a purpose. Glory to the Lord Jesus.

This man, sent by God, transported and directed there, finds the man reading Scripture but not understanding it. When Philip asks him, “Do you understand what you are reading?” he says, “How can I, unless someone explains it to me?” I imagine Philip rolling up his sleeves and saying, “All right, this is my job.” And he begins telling him that Jesus is the author of life, that the prophecy had been fulfilled, that the Messiah had already come, and that this Messiah could forgive his sins.

The Bible tells us in Acts 8:36: “As they went along the road, they came to some water, and the eunuch said, ‘Look, here is water. What prevents me from being baptized?’” This is amazing, church, because God is using a man or a woman to bring people what they cannot understand on their own. They may want God, but they do not understand God. But there is someone here who does understand. Glory to the Lord. We have not merely read about Him in a Bible; we have experienced Him. We have not merely walked into a church; we have had an experience with Almighty God. We do not only know the Bible; that God has also transformed us.

Oh, I still remember, brother, when I had religion but did not know who God was. But when I met someone who was a point of contact, I remember wanting to know that God. When someone makes contact, people want to be with you. They want to know about you and talk with you. I have an experience with the brother who evangelized me. Brother Enrique is like honey, because the flies are always around him. People just want to talk with him because they want to know what he has, what he does, how he lives, and how he deals with things. They may have Bibles in their homes, but they do not understand this. Someone needs to be a point of contact—someone who, when they speak, talks about what the Bible says and what the God of the Bible can do. Does anyone here know what the God of the Bible can do?

I remember going to a traditional church with pain and leaving with the same pain. Maybe my conscience felt calmer, but my problem was still there. But when I met someone who was a point of contact, brother, I came to the place where those people made contact with me. They were not harassing me to join their church; they simply lived knowing God, and when the subject came up, they talked to me. I was hungry. Is anyone hungry for God? Then the conversation began, and I wanted that God. They would stop when they needed to stop talking about the Bible because they did not want to offend me. What they wanted was for me to know that God, and I want that life. Does anyone want the life of Christ Jesus and to have that life?

Making contact is very important. How many mothers are here? Doctors know that when your baby is born, they need to place the baby on the mother’s chest. A baby has a strong desire to live after the impact of being born. The baby’s body changes shape during birth to come out, and the baby is beginning a new life, a different way of life. So as soon as the baby is born, the doctors immediately place the baby on the mother’s chest. The mother needs it, and the baby needs it. Later they can separate them, and the child can survive. They may not even want to come home or send us messages anymore, but God bless them. When babies are first born, they need that contact. The church is so important during these weeks: we need to make contact with people. We make contact with what is divine, with what is eternal. Glory to the Lord Jesus Christ.

People may read the Bible and still not understand it. But when someone knows and understands that God, when someone praises God for that Word, that is when we become a point of contact, brothers and sisters. People may read the Bible, but they do not understand it. They may have religion, but they do not have Christ. But when people make contact, we begin talking about Christ, and then Christ begins telling us what to do. We understand. How many understand, brother? I do not know everything. I enter a home, and when I do, I have to find out what the Holy Spirit wants to do in that home. What does God want to do through this phone call? God is the one who works. When we enter that home and begin talking with those people, we understand that Jesus wants to do something there—whether through an illness, a problem, or a need. It is a moment of contact, a moment when we tell them: Jesus can help you. Jesus is the author of life.

When the eunuch understands, he says, “Look, here is water. What prevents me from being baptized?” And you know the story in the Bible. Philip baptizes him, then God takes Philip away, and the eunuch continues on his way.

Beloved church, do not be sad when someone does not want to follow you. “Brother, they are not answering me. I am not going to do the evangelism cycle anymore; out of the 15 people I talk to, only two stay in the church.” Do not worry. We do not work for a building or an organization. We work because we make contact with people. We will tell them about Christ even if they do not come to church. We will tell them about Christ even if we are just passing through that place, because we will connect them with Christ Jesus.

Brothers and sisters, what would we be if that person who made contact with us had never existed? Where would we be if there had not been someone connected to God who spoke to us? Religion does not fill you. Religion does not fill you, but Jesus saves you and transforms you. Glory to the Lord. He brings transformation into your life.

Making contact is important. No matter how much voltage there is, if there is no point of contact, it will not turn on; it will not work. God is powerful. Someone say, “Amen.” He is Almighty, but for some tremendous and powerful reason, God dared to trust us. God told us, “You go; you will be the point of contact.” He breathed on them and gave them the Holy Spirit so that when we make contact, God can begin working in people and transforming them. We are that point of contact. God wants to work miracles, but He will use us. Throughout the Bible, God works through one of His points of contact. God uses one of His points of contact to go pray or declare a word so that He can work. Praise the Lord Jesus Christ.

What does someone who carries a message feel? What is it like to be someone who carries a message? In the Bible, in the days of the kings, we read about men who carried what God wanted to say to the people. We call them prophets, but you do not have to be a prophet to carry what God wants to say. What is it like to be someone who carries a message?

Elisha went to Damascus during the time of King Ben-Hadad, who was very sick. Ben-Hadad sent Hazael: “Go speak with the prophet and find out whether I will recover from this illness.” Brothers and sisters, what is it really like to be a messenger? People trust you with so many things, and sometimes you do not know what to do. But because you are connected to God, you declare a word and God confirms it, because God is in the mind and heart of the messenger. Sometimes people will not like what you tell them, but months or years later they will say, “You were right! The word you gave me came true.” The word the prophet gave him was: “Your king will recover, but afterward he will die.”

And listen to what happens. The Bible says that even though the prophet Elisha delivered the message, he began to weep, his heart was troubled, and he became sad, because God lets the messenger feel, through their experience, what that family will face. Is anyone following me? Does anyone know what I am preaching? You can sense that a young man, if he does not hold on to God, will end up in drug addiction, or that a young woman will end up in a bad situation. Does anyone know what I am preaching? How many intercessors are here? You look at that young woman and feel like telling her, “Do not keep going like this. You will end up in an institution; you will end up in a terrible place.” Does anyone know what I am preaching?

I look at the prophet, and when I see the prophet, I see those who carry a message. Sometimes we feel like crying when we see a situation, just as the prophet began to see, and he tells Hazael, “Something terrible is going to happen through you.” Hazael asks him, “Why are you crying? What is happening to you?” And he says, “You are going to devastate the people, mistreat the children, and kill the children—even the babies.” Somehow, God allowed the prophet of the Lord to see the future of what would happen with that man. The prophet began to see what was going to happen and that he would not be able to prevent it. He began to weep and feel the pain of what was going to take place.

What is it like to be a messenger? Someone who carries a message... We feel people’s pain when we speak to them about God and they reject the gospel. I know what will happen to a family when we visit them, tell them about Jesus, and they decide to push us away. Sometimes in prayer we cry and call out to the Lord: “Save this young woman. I do not know where her life is headed or what will happen, Lord.” In some way God reveals it to us; do not think we do not notice. Are there intercessors here?

Sometimes people try to convince you that everything is fine, but in reality everything is wrong, because their wife has already told you. Young people say, “Everything is fine,” but you can see it on their faces. I was not born yesterday, and God did not give me the testimony I have so I could keep it locked in a drawer. I know you are using drugs; I know you are struggling with pornography; I know you are struggling with the life you are living. If you do not leave that behind, you will end up there and suffer. When I see you, I want to cry and tell you, “Get out of that life.” Messengers are connected to what God feels for that person. Does anyone know what I am preaching here right now? Does anyone know?

We see a marriage that is suffering, and we know what may happen. Sometimes we want to say more and help them more. How many parents here want to spare their children the problems we had, and sometimes try to help them? We have cried so we could tell them, “Do not make the mistake I made.” But we thank God that He has us here with a purpose. Someone praise the Lord.

I do not know about you, but I cannot stay quiet, brothers and sisters. I believe that if there are people here who make contact, when we sit and talk with someone in the dining room, somewhere, or at a restaurant, I cannot just sit there eating. I have to talk to them about Jesus. I have to tell them God brought me here for a reason. There have been times, brothers and sisters, when we were sharing the gospel in a restaurant and the Lord gave us a word right there. I say to the waitress, “Do you know God?” Brother, I do not know if you know God, but when God moves us, and then we see the tears, she says, “My mother is praying for me. I need a church.” We are people who feel. How many feel it now? A person of God feels what is happening.

Even though we feel it, we cannot force people. But we can make sure that feeling does not stay there; it can move us to tell people what we feel. What does an intercessor, someone who makes contact, feel? They feel they cannot keep what they have to themselves. We have to give it to others. We have to speak to others about Jesus.

A woman who made contact, whose name we do not even know in the Bible, is known as the Samaritan woman. Let us read John 4 together. John 4:29 and 30. Look at what this woman says, because she did not keep it to herself. Read it with me. The first part says: “Come, see a man.” Come on, read it. One, two, three. I found someone who gave me the answer, and He can give it to you too. I found a place where God transformed me, and He can transform you too. God brought me to that place, and He can do the same for you.

People will criticize the person who makes contact. They will say, “Why do you only talk about Christ? I call you, and all you do is start talking to me about Jesus.” Take it easy, because that is just a moment people will go through. The day and the moment will come when they come to you, even if they reject you, because Jesus is a need of humanity. Glory to God.

Look at what verse 39 says: “Many of the Samaritans from that town believed in Him because of the woman’s testimony: ‘He told me everything I ever did.’”

Beloved church, an entire Samaritan city was reached and connected to God through one person—a woman who did not stay quiet. I said she did not stay quiet; she did not fill out a sheet. She said, “I am going to talk to everyone in the city.” And many believed in the Lord. Many men believed in the Lord because of this woman.
