Sermon transcript

Full transcript of the message preached by Eduardo García.

How are you all this afternoon? Amen. I didn’t hear much excitement. Well, it is always important, and it is a joy to be here and to worship, exalt, and praise our Lord Jesus Christ with our lips, our hands, and our whole being. Amen. We are in a very important week: visitation week. Last week was contact week, and next week—not the coming one, but the week after—will be invitation week. These are all part of our evangelism cycle. Amen. So the fifth week is the week for fasting. My brothers and sisters, during this visitation week, let us focus a little so we can understand this word and know more clearly what we are going to do when we visit. To visit primarily means going somewhere to get to know it, or going to see someone at home or at work, whether out of courtesy, friendship, or because there is a purpose for seeing that person. But every visit we have mentioned should have something behind it: an intention, an interest, a message, or an important purpose. That is why we go and make that visit. Amen. So, without further introduction, beloved church, allow me to preach and share the message titled “So My People Can Hear Me.” Why don’t you stand, and let us open our Bibles? We are going to read the Word of God in the book of Exodus, chapter 3, verse 2. Today we will focus on Exodus 3, verses 2 through 7, and then we will go a little farther down to verse 19. If you did not bring your Bible, our brothers will put it on the screen in a moment. Amen. The Word of God in Exodus chapter 3, verse 2, says: “There the angel of the Lord appeared to him.” Repeat after me: appeared—He visited him—“in flames of fire from within a bush. Moses saw that though the bush was on fire it did not burn up.” Stay standing and join me in prayer.

Prayer: Beloved Father, we love You and thank You because You visited us, because You called us, Lord, not to judge us but to forgive us, Father. We ask You, Lord, that at this moment, at this time, Your Holy Spirit would speak to Your people. May we hear Your Word, the message, and above all understand the purpose for which You have us here today, Father. We ask You, Lord, to use my brother’s lips and life as he translates, Father. May Your message get through and build us up. I pray, Lord, for everyone who is here today and was able to come, and also for those who are on their way. In the name of Jesus we pray. Amen.

We may sit down, beloved church. The message is “So My People Can Hear Me.” That is the title of this message. Are you ready, my sister, my beloved brother, to hear the Word of God? Exodus chapter 3, verse 3: “‘This is incredible,’ Moses said to himself. ‘Why doesn’t this bush burn up? I have to go over and see it more closely.’” I imagine Moses spent a long time looking at that blazing fire, at that bush, and became curious enough to approach it, to go and visit that blazing fire more closely to see why it did not burn up. In verse 4, when the Lord saw Moses approaching, He called out, “Moses! Moses!” “Here I am,” he replied. Verse 5 says, “Do not come any closer,” the Lord warned. “Take off your sandals, for you are standing on holy ground.” There is something important in this verse, beloved church. What was God’s reason or purpose in visiting Moses? Let us see what the Word of God says in the following verse: “I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.” When Moses heard this, he covered his face because he was afraid to look at God. In verse 7 the Lord said: “I have indeed seen the misery of my people in Egypt. I have heard them crying out because of their slave drivers, and I am concerned about their suffering.” Amen. The Lord hears; the Lord sees our afflictions. Amen.

We were saying that visits carry a message, a reason, a purpose. We make those visits because we want to bring something.

In the book of Exodus, chapter 19, verse 9, let us see the purpose of the Lord’s visit to Moses in that bush: “The Lord said to Moses, ‘I am going to come to you in a dense cloud, so that the people will hear me speaking with you and will always put their trust in you.’” Repeat after me: so that my people may hear. “And Moses told the Lord what the people had said.” Beloved church, God speaks directly to us often and in many ways. Amen. Is there someone who can shout a loud “Amen”? Glory to God. Be loud when it is for God. Usually, when we visit someone or contact them, call them on the phone, or perhaps send a text or an email, it is because we have a reason, something we want to share with that person. We are in a week when we will visit homes. This is our week to visit and conclude this visitation week in our evangelism cycle. But we are not going just for the sake of going. We must focus and understand the specific reason and purpose for visiting these homes.

Let us go to the Word of God in the book of Ezekiel, chapter 37, verse 13. The Word of God says: “Then you, my people, will know that I am the Lord, when I open your graves and bring you up from them.” And that is how it will be, beloved church: the day will come, the day of final judgment, when the Word of God says we will all be called; we will all be invited. But specifically this morning, our Lord is speaking to His people to tell them that there is a much greater and more remarkable change: a person can be raised from the grave of spiritual death and brought into the light of spiritual life. That is the purpose of today’s visit to this church. Glory to God.

Beloved church, no one can do this except the living God, our Lord and giver of life. We cannot do it, beloved church. The Lord has not only visited us; He has also given us His message—not so we will remain silent, but so we can be the contact, the means God uses to go out, make contact, visit those homes, and help people come to know Him. Those who are still buried in spiritual death and enslaved by their weaknesses, drugs, addictions, or depression can find purpose through the means God is using: you and me.

But how will we go to those people? We cannot go alone. We need the Spirit of God to go with us. Amen.

Let us go to the Word of God in the book of Ephesians, chapter 4, verses 15 and 16. This is what the Lord wants us to do when we visit those homes: “Instead, speaking the truth in love, we will grow to become in every respect the mature body of Him who is the head, that is, Christ.” Verse 16 says: “From Him the whole body, joined and held together by every supporting ligament, grows and builds itself up in love, as each part does its work.” Amen. Amen, church, because many of us will be the ones who visit, while others will be the ones who receive us—so that my people may hear me.

What did our Lord Jesus do so that we could hear Him? The Word tells us in John, chapter 1, verse 14. Listen carefully; this is important: “The Word became flesh and dwelt among us.” Repeat after me: dwelt, came, visited. “The Word became flesh and made His dwelling among us. We have seen His glory, the glory of the one and only Son, who came from the Father, full of grace and truth.” Glory to God. So, beloved church, beloved brother, when we go to visit, we will go filled with God’s grace and truth. Amen. Glory to God. Hallelujah. Wow. When you visit people this week, we will go filled with His grace and truth, because our Lord has visited us, contacted us, and called us—not only because He wants to bring us salvation and eternal life. No, no, no. Beloved church, our Lord has also contacted us so that we can be the contact, the means the Lord will use to go, visit, and share His Word—the message of repentance and salvation. Glory to God. Oh, I feel like time has gotten away from me. I don’t know, but I don’t want to bore you or put you to sleep. I had not prepared this message. Usually, you prepare ahead of time to share a message, but this time I received the notice a few days ago. It was almost 11 at night when I saw the message, and I thought it was for October, but when I looked carefully, it was for September 7. At that moment I told the Lord—I was about to reply to the message and say, “I can’t.” But I remembered that some years ago I told the Lord to use my life, to send me, to take me wherever He wanted, according to His will and not mine. So at 11 at night I took my Bible, began to read it, and said, “Lord, speak, for Your servant is listening.” This Word comes from the Lord, our God. Do not set your eyes on people, because people will fail. Instead, look to the One who never fails, our Lord Jesus Christ. Amen. Honor and glory belong to our Lord Jesus.

The apostle Paul gives us very important advice in Colossians 4:6. The Word of God says: “Let your conversation be always full of grace, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how to answer everyone.” The apostle Paul is telling us that we should support and welcome the kingdom’s workers, just as they offered comfort and worked to expand the kingdom of God. Many of us are saying, “Lord, use us; use my life.” And this verse—sorry, in Colossians—specifically highlights Jesus, who is called Justus. A little farther down, verses 9 and 10 mention Mark and Aristarchus as people who worked and were a great comfort to Paul and to the kingdom of God, emphasizing the importance of welcoming them when they visit homes, communities, and households.

At one time, I was someone who received visitors. I received the Word of God. Someone brought it to my house, to my home. Perhaps I was not the best host; perhaps I did not welcome those brothers, those couples, very well, right? Maybe it was because I did not know this Word, had not studied it deeply, and had not thought enough about what God wants—about the reason the Lord visited Moses in that blazing bush. Now that I understand, the Word of God says we should freely give what we have freely received.

So if God has visited us so that we may do His will, here is the question: When was the last time you asked the Lord, “What is Your will for my life right now, Lord?” When was the last time you asked God, “How do You want me to respond to this situation, this problem, this attack, this anger, this envy, Lord?” Glory to God. The Lord is good and faithful, church. Many times we will feel defeated, trampled, perhaps tired and without strength, wanting to throw in the towel.

Stand up, church. I want us to learn and listen together to what our Lord’s will is for our lives. Let me share six points with you. 1. That we trust in Him. Let us not set our eyes or our trust on people, because we will fail; instead, let us keep our eyes and our trust on the One who can do all things, the Sovereign, the King of kings. 2. That He be our guide. Let us seek God’s presence, read His Word, and let Him guide us, not our flesh. 3. He wants what is best for us. He always has. It is not true that you went to Him. It is not true that I went to Him. The Lord visited me. The Lord came into my life; He came to Moses in a bush. At my home, the Lord used a contact—some couples who brought His Word—and that changed my life and my household. Just as the Lord will use you, do not be afraid, because you go with God’s grace and truth. 4. He does not want us to suffer or be miserable. 5. He wants us to feel fulfilled. 6. His will, His purpose, is for us to surrender our lives to Him. This point is neither more nor less important, because they are all important. Do you know why people do not surrender their will to God? It is very simple: they do not believe God has a purpose for them.

Altar call. This afternoon I would like to make two altar calls. Here is the bush and the blazing fire. Amen. If you believe it, shout a loud “Amen.” Hallelujah. The first call is for everyone who believes God has a purpose for their lives. Come closer, like Moses, to receive what God has for you today. The second call is for every visitor, every friend, everyone who has heard this Word today and wants to give themselves over and surrender to God’s will today. Come, beloved church, do not hold back. Let us come to the altar. God has visited us. His presence is here. Friend, visitor, do not think—perhaps you came with a different purpose, with a different idea. Perhaps you came like Moses, out of curiosity, but today the Lord is speaking to your heart. Today the Lord is speaking to your life. Today the Lord wants you to know and understand His will and the purpose for which He brought you here today. Let us close our eyes, church. Let us speak with our Lord. Let us connect our spirit with His and say to Him:

Prayer of surrender: Father, we love You. Father, we thank You because You came, because You visited us, Lord, not to judge us but to forgive our sins. You came, Lord, not to point at us but to embrace us, Father. You came into our lives to change everything, Lord, everything in our lives. For so many years, Lord, we walked through life, through the world, doing worthless things, doing our own will, rejecting Your love, wasting our lives, and affecting the lives of our households, our families, and our children. But Your grace and mercy have reached us. And today we are here, Lord, bowed down in Your presence. Use our lives, Lord. We want to be that contact, that means, Lord, to go out and visit the homes and families still living in darkness, still blinded, Lord, by hatred, envy, and addictions. Come on, church, cry out to the Lord. Cry out and be filled with His presence. Lord, forgive me, Father. Forgive us, Lord. Forgive us, Lord. Here we are, surrendered to You, acknowledging You, Lord, as our Father. Here we are, Lord, with humble hearts before You. Do Your will in our minds, Lord, and use us. Use us, Father, according to Your will, because You want what is best—the best for Your children, the best for Your people. Thank You, Lord. Thank You, Lord. We glorify You, Lord. We exalt Your name forever.
